## Key Concepts
- **Mufsidaat al-Salah** — acts that nullify the prayer, totalling nine, all occurring voluntarily through the choice of the Musalli
- **Kalam al-Naas (human speech)** — the central criterion; anything that amounts to human communication of meaning within Salah invalidates it
- **Sahin / Khata / Nisyan** — three distinct categories of non-intentional action (oversight, mistake, forgetfulness), each defined precisely
- **Huruf al-Zawaid** — extra/supplementary letters in Arabic (gathered in the phrase اليوم تنساه), relevant to Imam Abu Yusuf's opinion on articulated sounds
- **Lqmah (prompting)** — correcting a reciter's mistake; rules differ sharply depending on whether the recipient is one's own Imam or someone else
- **Istihsan** — juristic preference, invoked to permit giving lqmah to one's own Imam despite the general prohibition on speech
- **Niyyah (intention)** — taken into consideration by the Tarafayn (Imam Abu Hanifa and Imam Muhammad) in certain nullifiers, but not by Imam Abu Yusuf

## Detailed Notes

### Overview: The Nine Nullifiers
The text lists nine nullifiers (mufsidaat) of Salah, all of which occur voluntarily through the choice of the Musalli. The nine are:
1. Speech (Kalam)
2. Distress/Sorrow (al-Jaza' wa al-Ta'assuf)
3. Clearing the throat (al-Tanhanuh)
4. Responding to a sneeze (Tashmiit al-Aatis)
5. Giving lqmah to someone other than one's own Imam (al-Istiftah)
6. Saying La ilaha ill-Allah / Inna lillah with the intention of response
7. Beginning an act other than the one currently being performed
8. Excessive movement (al-'Amal al-Kathir)
9. Receiving a lqmah from someone outside of Salah (al-Talaqqi min al-Kharij)

### Nullifier 1: Speech (Kalam)

#### The Basic Ruling
If a person speaks in his Salah — meaning before sitting for the duration of Tashahhud — his prayer is nullified. (Speech after sitting for the duration of Tashahhud was discussed previously under exiting the Salah through one's own action, which is a valid exit.)

The text mentions two categories: intentionally (ʿamdan) and due to oversight (sahiyan). The term *sahiyan* in the ibarah encompasses three sub-types:
- **ʿAmdan** — intentionally
- **Sahiyan** — oversight (the person becomes aware with the slightest reminder)
- **Khata'** — mistake (one does not become aware through a reminder, or only becomes aware after effort; the act occurred while one was not forgetful of being in Salah, but it happened without intent)
- **Nisyan** — forgetfulness (something previously perceived/known fully leaves the memory and must be re-acquired)

In all four cases, according to the Hanafi position (and one narration of Imam Ahmad), the Salah is nullified.

#### Distinguishing Sahw, Khata', and Nisyan
These three are distinct:
- **Sahw (oversight):** Leaves the memory momentarily; recalled with the slightest reminder. The information was in the mind but briefly absent.
- **Nisyan (forgetfulness):** Leaves the memory entirely; the person must re-acquire the knowledge — it does not simply come back.
- **Khata' (mistake):** The information was never absent from memory; the person knew they were praying but the action happened anyway (e.g., words slipped out involuntarily while fully aware of being in Salah).

There are thus three levels: oversight → forgetfulness → mistake, in terms of how easily awareness is restored.

#### Scholarly Disagreement (Ikhtilaf)
- **Hanafi position (one view of Imam Ahmad):** All four cases nullify the Salah.
- **Imam al-Shafi'i, Imam Malik, and Imam Ahmad (in another narration):** Agree that intentional speech nullifies Salah, but disagree regarding *khata'* and *nisyan* — holding that in those two cases the Salah is not nullified.

#### Dalil of Each Side
- **Imam al-Shafi'i's dalil:** The well-known hadith, *"Liability has been lifted from my Ummah in the case of mistake and forgetfulness"* (Ruf'ia 'an ummati al-khata' wa al-nisyan).
- **Hanafi dalil:** The hadith of the Prophet ﷺ: *"In this prayer of ours, no part of human speech is valid for it; it is only tasbih, tahlil, and the recitation of the Quran."*
- **Hanafi response to Shafi'i's hadith:** The hadith about lifting liability refers to the removal of *sin* only, not to the nullification of the act. A person who speaks by mistake will not be sinful, but the Salah is still invalidated.

#### The Qiyas of Imam al-Shafi'i and the Hanafi Rebuttal
Imam al-Shafi'i made a qiyas: just as *salam* (which is also a form of speech/kalam) done forgetfully or mistakenly does not break Salah, similarly *kalam* done forgetfully or mistakenly should not break Salah.

**Hanafi rebuttal:** This qiyas is fasid (invalid) because salam is a dual-natured word — it is *both* kalam (speech) and dhikr (remembrance). Therefore:
- If salam is said **intentionally**, it is treated as speech and breaks Salah.
- If salam is said **forgetfully or mistakenly**, it is treated as dhikr and does not break Salah.

Regular kalam, however, has only one nature — it is purely speech. You cannot apply the same half-half treatment to it. Therefore, the qiyas of kalam upon salam is not valid.

### Nullifier 2: Distress and Sorrow (al-Jaza' wa al-Ta'assuf)

#### The Basic Ruling
If a person **groans**, **sighs**, or **cries** in Salah, and his cries are loud enough to produce distinct, articulated letters, then:

**Scenario A — Due to remembrance of Jannah or Jahannam:**
- The Salah is **not** broken.
- Reason: This is a sign of increased khushu' (devotion) and deep contemplation.

**Scenario B — Due to pain or distress:**
- The Salah **is** broken — *but only if the person was able to control it and chose not to*.
- If the pain is so extreme that the person cannot control the sounds, it does not break the Salah. In that case, such sounds are treated like sneezing, coughing, clearing the throat (when necessary), and yawning — all of which, even if they produce letters, do not break Salah due to necessity (darura).
- Reason for breaking (when controllable): Such sounds amount to an expression of anguish and regret (*izhar al-jaza' wa al-ta'assuf*), which is a form of human speech, and human speech breaks Salah.

#### The Masalah of Articulated Letters (Huruf)
A detailed discussion arose on when produced sounds amount to speech:

**Imam Abu Yusuf's position:**
- If the sound is composed of **one letter** → does not break Salah regardless of what letter.
- If composed of **two letters**:
  - If both (or at least one) are from the *huruf al-zawaid* → does not break Salah.
  - If both are *not* from the huruf al-zawaid → breaks Salah.
- If composed of **three letters** → breaks Salah in all cases, regardless of whether the letters are zawaid or not.

**The Huruf al-Zawaid** are gathered in the phrase: **اليوم تنساه** (Alif, Lam, Ya, Waw, Meem, Ta, Nun, Sin, Alif, Ha).

Example: "Ah" (آه) in Arabic = Alif + Ha, both zawaid → does not break Salah (per Abu Yusuf). "Ooh" (أوه) = three letters → breaks Salah (per Abu Yusuf).

**Position of al-Tarafayn (Imam Abu Hanifa and Imam Muhammad):**
- The zawaid/asl distinction is not the operative criterion.
- What matters is whether the sound **communicates a meaning** according to the customary usage of people (*'urf*).
- If even one or two letters communicate a meaning in common usage, then it breaks Salah — regardless of whether those letters are zawaid.
- Example: In common usage (e.g., in Pakistan), saying "ايه" (eh) communicates surprise/displeasure — even though both letters are zawaid — and would therefore break Salah according to al-Tarafayn, but not according to Abu Yusuf.
- Three-letter words: al-Tarafayn also agree these break Salah, because three letters will almost always convey a meaning.
- **This is the correct and adopted opinion.**

Reason why Abu Yusuf's opinion is not strong: human speech follows the communication of meaning, not the classification of letters as zawaid or asl. Meaning can be conveyed even with all-zawaid letters.

### Nullifier 3: Clearing the Throat (al-Tanhanuh)

#### The Basic Ruling
If a person clears his throat in Salah **without any excuse** (i.e., he was not compelled to do so), **and** this leads to the articulation of distinct letters, then — in the opinion of al-Tarafayn — the Salah is necessarily invalidated.

If he clears his throat **due to an excuse** (e.g., phlegm, need to continue recitation clearly, to prevent a cough from erupting), then it is **overlooked (*'afw*)** — just like sneezing. Even if it produces letters, it does not invalidate the Salah.

Key distinction:
- If he can control it and still clears his throat → Salah is broken if letters are produced.
- If he cannot control it (extreme necessity) → treated like sneezing; Salah is not broken.

Valid excuses include: removing phlegm blocking clear recitation, preventing a worse cough, needing to breathe properly for continued recitation, or indicating one's presence to someone in danger (though in such situations reciting tasbih aloud is preferable).

Note: Merely clearing the throat without it reaching the level of articulating letters — even without an excuse — is a lesser matter, though still disliked.

### Nullifier 4: Responding to a Sneeze — Tashmiit al-Aatis

#### The Basic Ruling
If a person is in Salah and someone nearby sneezes, and the person in Salah responds by saying **"Yarhamukallah"**, his prayer is nullified.

- **Al-Tarafayn:** Salah is invalidated.
- **Imam Abu Yusuf:** Salah is not invalidated.

**Reason (al-Tarafayn):** "Yarhamukallah" is used in customary communication between people (*yajri fi mukhatabat al-naas*) — it is a response/answer (jawab). Even though it is also a du'a, the context makes it an interpersonal exchange, and interpersonal speech nullifies Salah.

#### The Role of Niyyah (Intention)
- **Al-Tarafayn** take the niyyah into account: if the person uttered the words with the intention of *jawab* (response), it is treated as response and breaks Salah. If with the intention of *ithna'* (praise/glorification), then...
- **Imam Abu Yusuf** does not look at the niyyah; he always treats "Yarhamukallah" as a glorification/praise (*thana'*), not as a response, and therefore holds it does not break Salah.

#### Important Distinctions
- **"Yarhamukallah"** → breaks Salah (per al-Tarafayn), because it is a customary response to a sneeze with the *kaf al-khitab* (pronoun addressing the other person).
- **"Alhamdulillah"** said by either the sneezer or the one who heard the sneeze → does **not** break Salah, because this is not deemed a customary response (*lam yata'aaraf jawaban*); the customary response is "Yarhamukallah," not "Alhamdulillah." Hence it is not treated as human speech.
- **"Yarhamuniyallah"** (asking mercy for oneself) → does **not** break Salah, because this is not a customary response either; custom dictates saying it to the *other* person.

A narration from Imam Abu Hanifa (mentioned in al-Muhit) states that when a person sneezes in Salah, he should praise Allah inwardly in his heart without moving his tongue; if he moves his tongue, the prayer becomes invalid — but this is noted as another opinion, with the position in the main text being more correct.

### Nullifier 5: Giving Lqmah to Someone Other than One's Own Imam (al-Istiftah)

#### Definition
**Istiftah / Lqmah:** Prompting (correcting/assisting) the recitation of someone who is stuck.

If a person in Salah gives a lqmah (correction/prompt) to **anyone other than his own Imam**, whether that other person is in Salah or not, his own Salah is invalidated. The other person's Salah is also invalidated if they are in Salah and they accept the lqmah.

**Reason:** This amounts to *ta'lim wa ta'allum* (teaching and being taught), which is a category of human speech.

#### Exception: Giving Lqmah to One's Own Imam
Giving a lqmah to **one's own Imam** does **not** invalidate the Salah. This is based on **istihsan** (juristic preference).

**Reason:** The Imam is under a compulsion to correct his Salah. If the Muqtadi does not correct him, the Imam's mistake could render everyone's Salah invalid (since, in the Hanafi madhhab, if the Imam's Salah is invalid, the Muqtadis' Salah is also invalid). Therefore, correcting the Imam is a necessity, and the lqmah in this case is treated as an **act of prayer**, not as speech — similar to how salam at the end of prayer is both speech and an act of Salah.

#### The Niyyah When Giving Lqmah to the Imam
The correct view is that when the Muqtadi gives lqmah to his Imam, he should make the **intention of giving lqmah** — not the intention of recitation (*qira'ah*).

**Reason:** Giving lqmah is a permitted exemption (*rukh sah*) in this case; recitation by the Muqtadi is actually forbidden. Therefore, the intention must be for the permitted act (lqmah), not the forbidden one (qira'ah).
