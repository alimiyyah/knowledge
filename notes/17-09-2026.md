# Nullifiers of Salah (Mufsidāt al-Ṣalāh)
**Date:** 17-09-2026
**Source transcript:** whisper

## Summary
This class covered the nine nullifiers (mufsidāt) of Ṣalāh in detail, working through each one systematically with reference to the primary text (matn), hashiyah, and positions of the various Imams. The session focused heavily on the first five nullifiers — speech, distress/sorrow, clearing the throat, responding to a sneeze, and giving a laqmah (prompt) to someone other than one's own Imam — before briefly introducing the remaining four.

## Key Concepts
- **Mufsidāt al-Ṣalāh** — things that nullify the prayer; nine in total, all occurring voluntarily through the choice of the muṣallī
- **Kālam al-nās (human speech)** — the central criterion; anything that constitutes human speech (communication of meaning) nullifies Ṣalāh
- **Sāhī / Khataʾ / Nisyān** — three distinct categories of non-intentional action, each with different legal implications
- **Laqmah (استفتاح)** — prompting/correcting a reciter; permissible for one's own Imam, but nullifying if done for anyone else (per the fatwā position)
- **Ḥurūf al-Zawāʾid** — extra/supplementary letters in Arabic, relevant to Imam Abū Yūsuf's rule on whether sounds produced in Ṣalāh constitute invalidating speech
- **Niyyah (intention)** — taken into consideration by the Ṭarafayn when determining whether an utterance is glorification or response; not considered by Imam Abū Yūsuf

## Detailed Notes

### Introduction
The chapter covers the nine nullifiers of Ṣalāh (mufsidāt). These are things that occur voluntarily through the choice of the muṣallī. The teacher noted that the nullifier of salām (exiting by salām) had already been covered previously.

### Nullifier 1: Speech (al-Kalām)

**The ruling:**
If a person speaks in his Ṣalāh — before sitting for the duration of Tashahhud — his prayer is nullified. This applies in four scenarios:
1. Intentionally (ʿāmidan)
2. Due to oversight (sāhiyan)
3. Due to mistake (khataʾan)
4. Due to forgetfulness (nisyānan)

The word *sāhiyan* in the ʿibārah encompasses all three of the non-intentional categories (oversight, mistake, forgetfulness), making four total.

**Distinction between the three non-intentional states:**
- **Sāhī (oversight):** Something that leaves the mind momentarily but returns with the slightest reminder. It leaves the *quwwat al-mudrikah* (perceptive faculty) but not the deeper memory.
- **Nisyān (forgetfulness):** Something that was previously known leaves the imagination and memory entirely, requiring a new acquisition (*kasb jadīd*) to recover it. It does not simply come back — it must be relearned.
- **Khataʾ (mistake):** The information has not left the memory; rather, the person acted wrongly while still aware. Example: knowing one is fasting but accidentally swallowing water while gargling. Or: in Ṣalāh, something slips out of one's mouth even though one knew one was praying.

These are three distinct levels: Sāhī → Nisyān → Khataʾ (in terms of increasing difficulty of recovery/awareness).

**The ikhtilāf:**
- **Ḥanafī position (one view of Imam Aḥmad):** All four cases nullify Ṣalāh.
- **Imam al-Shāfiʿī, and one narration from Imam Mālik and Imam Aḥmad:** Agree that intentional speech and oversight nullify Ṣalāh, but disagree in the cases of *mistake* and *forgetfulness* — in these two cases, they say the Ṣalāh is not nullified.

**Imam al-Shāfiʿī's dalīl:**
The well-known hadith: *"Liability has been lifted from my Ummah in the case of mistake and forgetfulness"* (Ḥadīth al-Rafʿ).

**Ḥanafī response:**
Our dalīl is the hadith of the Prophet ﷺ: *"In this prayer of ours, no part of human speech is valid for it; it is only tasbīḥ, tahlīl, and recitation of the Qurʾān."* As for the hadith cited by Imam al-Shāfiʿī, we interpret it as referring to the removal of *sin* (ithm) only — not the removal of the legal consequence of nullification.

**Imam al-Shāfiʿī's qiyās and its refutation:**
Imam al-Shāfiʿī draws an analogy: just as salām done forgetfully or by mistake does not break Ṣalāh (and salām is also a form of speech/kalām), so too kalām done forgetfully or by mistake should not break Ṣalāh.

The Ḥanafī refutation: This qiyās is *fāsid* (invalid). Salām has a dual nature — it is both speech (kalām) and a form of remembrance (dhikr). Therefore:
- If salām is said *intentionally*, it is treated as speech → breaks Ṣalāh.
- If salām is said *forgetfully or mistakenly*, it is treated as dhikr → does not break Ṣalāh.

Kalām, by contrast, is purely speech — it has no dhikr dimension. You cannot apply the same "half-half" treatment to it. That is why the qiyās of kalām upon salām is not valid.

### Nullifier 2: Distress and Sorrow (al-Jazaʿ wa al-Taʾassuf)

**The ruling:**
If a person groans, sighs, or cries in Ṣalāh, and his crying is loud enough to result in the articulation of distinct letters, there are two scenarios:

1. **If it is due to the mention of Jannah or Jahannam** → Does *not* break Ṣalāh. This indicates increased khushūʿ (devotion).
2. **If it is due to pain or distress** → *Does* break Ṣalāh (if controllable). The reason: this amounts to an expression of anguish and regret (*iẓhār al-jazaʿ wa al-taʾassuf*), which is a form of human speech, and human speech breaks Ṣalāh.

**Exception for uncontrollable pain:**
If the pain is so severe that the person cannot control themselves, these groans and sighs are treated like sneezing, coughing, clearing the throat, and yawning — even if they produce letters, they do not break Ṣalāh due to necessity (ḍarūrah). The person must still try to suppress the sounds as much as possible.

**The masʾalah of articulated letters (ḥurūf):**

There is an ikhtilāf between **Imam Abū Yūsuf** and **al-Ṭarafayn** (Imam Abū Ḥanīfah and Imam Muḥammad).

**Imam Abū Yūsuf's rule:**
The prayer is or is not invalidated based on the number of letters in the sound and whether they are from the *ḥurūf al-zawāʾid* (supplementary letters). The ḥurūf al-zawāʾid are all gathered in the mnemonic phrase: **اليوم تنساه** (alif, lām, yāʾ, wāw, mīm, tāʾ, nūn, sīn, alif, hāʾ).

- **1 letter:** Does not invalidate Ṣalāh regardless.
- **2 letters:** If both are from ḥurūf al-zawāʾid, or at least one is → does *not* invalidate. If neither is from ḥurūf al-zawāʾid → *does* invalidate.
- **3 letters:** Always invalidates, regardless of whether they are zawāʾid or not.

*Example:* "Āh" (آه) = alif + hāʾ → both are zawāʾid → does not invalidate (per Abū Yūsuf). "Ūḥ" (أوه) = three letters → always invalidates.

**Al-Ṭarafayn's rule:**
The criterion is not the zawāʾid distinction but whether the sound *communicates a meaning* according to common usage (ʿurf). Human speech is defined by communication of meaning, achievable with any number of letters. Even two letters that are both zawāʾid can communicate meaning and thus break Ṣalāh.

*Example:* Saying "إيه" in Pakistan is used to express astonishment — this communicates a meaning. Even though both letters are from the zawāʾid, per al-Ṭarafayn this would break Ṣalāh. Per Abū Yūsuf it would not, since one letter is from the zawāʾid.

*Example:* Saying "من" (from) — this is a complete meaning and would break Ṣalāh according to al-Ṭarafayn.

*Example:* In Ṣalāh, if someone utters a sound expressing annoyance (e.g., "tch!") that communicates a meaning by common usage — this breaks Ṣalāh.

The teacher noted Imam Abū Yūsuf's rule was criticised by al-Ṭarafayn as not being a strong argument (*lā yaqwā*), because speech is evaluated by usage and the communication of meaning — not by the grammatical classification of letters.

### Nullifier 3: Clearing the Throat (al-Tanḥnuḥ)

**The ruling:**
If a person clears his throat:
- **Without an excuse (bilā ʿudhr)** — i.e., he was not compelled to do so — *and* this leads to the articulation of distinct letters → the prayer is nullified (according to al-Ṭarafayn).
- **With an excuse** (e.g., phlegm, need to continue recitation clearly, to prevent a cough) → it is overlooked (*ʿafw*), treated like sneezing. Even if it produces letters, it does not break Ṣalāh.

**Key distinctions:**
- If he clears his throat without an excuse but it does *not* produce letters → does not invalidate.
- If he clears his throat with an excuse and it *does* produce letters → still does not invalidate (due to necessity).
- The concern is: clearing the throat without excuse, to the level that letters are produced.

**Examples of valid excuses:**
- Phlegm blocking the throat.
- Need to recite clearly as Imam (people cannot follow otherwise).
- To prevent a worse cough from breaking out.
- To alert children or someone in danger while in Ṣalāh (though doing tasbīḥ loudly is the preferred method).

### Nullifier 4: Responding to a Sneeze (Tashmiyt al-ʿĀṭis)

**The ruling:**
If someone sneezes and a person in Ṣalāh says *"yarḥamukallāh"* in response, his prayer is nullified.

- **Al-Ṭarafayn:** Nullifies — because *yarḥamukallāh* is used in communication between people (it is a customary response/jawāb), and therefore constitutes human speech.
- **Imam Abū Yūsuf:** Does *not* nullify — he treats it as thanāʾ (praise/glorification) regardless of intention.

**Al-Ṭarafayn's reasoning:**
They take the *niyyah* (intention) into consideration. If the word was uttered as a response (*jawāb*), it is treated as speech. The word *yarḥamukallāh* is customarily used as a response, so it is treated as such — just like how *yarḥamukallāh* is treated as a response to a sneeze rather than as glorification, even though it contains praise of Allah.

**Distinction — saying "al-ḥamdulillāh":**
If the person sneezing or someone who hears it says *"al-ḥamdulillāh"* → does *not* nullify Ṣalāh. Reason: *al-ḥamdulillāh* is not the customary response to a sneeze — it is not deemed a *jawāb* by convention. Therefore it is not treated as human speech.

**Further distinction — saying "yarḥamunīallāh":**
If a person in Ṣalāh says *"yarḥamunīallāh"* (mercy on me) instead → does *not* nullify. Reason: this is not a customary response to a sneeze; custom does not recognise it as a jawāb.

**Narration from Imam Abū Ḥanīfah (from al-Muḥīt):**
When one sneezes in Ṣalāh, one should praise Allah inwardly in the heart without moving the tongue. If the tongue is moved, the prayer becomes invalid. The teacher noted this is an alternative view; the position in the matn is the more correct one.

### Nullifier 5: Giving a Laqmah (Prompt) to Someone Other Than One's Own Imam (al-Istiftāḥ)

**Definition:**
*Istiftāḥ* means giving a laqmah (prompting/correcting a reciter who is stuck) to someone other than one's own Imam, whether that other person is in Ṣalāh or not.

**The ruling:**
If a person in Ṣalāh corrects or prompts someone who is not his Imam — whether that person is in Ṣalāh or not — his own prayer is nullified. Reason: this constitutes teaching and instruction (*taʿlīm wa taʿallum*), which is a form of human speech.

**Condition — intention to recite:**
If, at the moment of giving the laqmah, the person *intends to recite* (e.g., he had just finished Sūrat al-Fātiḥah and naturally continued into the next verse which happened to correct the other person), then it is *not* counted as a laqmah and does not nullify his Ṣalāh.
If he did not intend to recite — he jumped from his place in recitation to fix the other person — then it *does* nullify his Ṣalāh.

**Ikhtilāf between Imam Muḥammad's two books:**
- **Kitāb al-Aṣl (al-Mabsūṭ):** Imam Muḥammad stipulates *repetition* — the Ṣalāh is only nullified if the laqmah is repeated multiple times. Minor/single prompting is overlooked (ʿafw).
- **Al-Jāmiʿ al-Ṣaghīr:** Even a single laqmah nullifies the Ṣalāh, because even one instance constitutes human speech.
- **The fatwā** is upon the position of al-Jāmiʿ al-Ṣaghīr — even once is invalidating. (وهو الأصح)

**Giving laqmah to one's own Imam:**
This does *not* nullify the Ṣalāh. This is based on *istiḥsān* (juristic preference). The reason: the Muqtadī is under a compulsion to fix the Imam's Ṣalāh. In Ḥanafī fiqh, the validity of the Muqtadī's Ṣalāh is tied to the validity of the Imam's Ṣalāh. If the Imam makes an error that could break everyone's Ṣalāh and no one corrects him, all their prayers become invalid. Therefore, this act is not treated as speech but as an act of Ṣalāh (in meaning).

**Niyyah when giving laqmah to Imam:**
When giving the laqmah, the Muqtadī should make the *intention of giving laqmah*, not the intention of recitation. This is the correct (ṣaḥīḥ) view. Reason: giving laqmah to the Imam is the exemption granted to him; recitation on his part (as a Muqtadī) is not permitted. The intention should correspond to the exemption.

**What if the laqmah given is wrong?**
If a Muqtadī gave an incorrect laqmah:
- If the Imam *did not take it* → only the Muqtadī's Ṣalāh is affected (potentially nullified if it was done without necessity and communicated meaning).
- If the Imam *took it* → per the matn of Imam al-Ghaznawī, both the Muqtadī's and the Imam's Ṣalāh would be rendered invalid (due to giving and accepting without necessity).

**The fatwā position (from Durr al-Mukhtār):**
Correcting one's own Imam does *not* invalidate the Ṣalāh under any circumstance — neither for the one correcting nor for the Imam accepting — in any scenario. This is the most correct (aṣaḥḥ) opinion.

**Imam al-Ghaznawī's guidance:**
It is essential that:
1. The Muqtadī not be hasty in giving laqmah.
2. The Imam not solicit laqmah from the congregation.
3. If the Imam has completed the minimum recitation (one āyah per Imam Abū Ḥanīfah; three āyāt per the Ṣāḥibayn), he should proceed to rukūʿ rather than wait for a laqmah.
4. Alternatively, the Imam should move to another verse he can recite without needing a prompt.

### Digression: Saying Lā ilāha illallāh / Istirjāʿ in Ṣalāh

**Lā ilāha illallāh said in Ṣalāh:**
- **Al-Ṭarafayn:** Take the niyyah into consideration. If intended as a *jawāb* (response to something), it breaks Ṣalāh. If intended as a *notification* that one is in Ṣalāh, it does not break Ṣalāh.
- **Imam Abū Yūsuf:** Does not consider niyyah — always treats it as thanāʾ/glorification, so it never breaks Ṣalāh.

**If the intention is to inform someone he is in Ṣalāh:** Does *not* break Ṣalāh by *ijmāʿ* (consensus). Dalīl: The hadith of the Prophet ﷺ — *"When some incident befalls one of you in Ṣalāh, let him glorify his Lord."* Tasbīḥ includes: سبحان الله، الحمد لله، الله أكبر، لا إله إلا الله.

**Istirjāʿ (saying إن لله وإنا إليه راجعون):**
The same ikhtilāf applies as for *lā ilāha illallāh*:
- Al-Ṭarafayn: niyyah is considered — if used as a response, it breaks Ṣalāh.
- Imam Abū Yūsuf: always treated as glorification, does not break Ṣalāh.

### Nullifier 7: Commencing a Different Prayer While in Ṣalāh (al-Shurūʿ fī Ghayr Mā Huwa fīh)

**The ruling:**
If a person has prayed one rakʿah of Ẓuhr, then while standing for the second rakʿah forms a new intention (in his heart) and says *"Allāhu Akbar"* (without raising the hands) for ʿAṣr or a nafl Ṣalāh:
- His Ẓuhr is rendered invalid.
- His new ʿAṣr Ṣalāh is valid and he continues it from there (praying four rakʿāt).

**The qāʿidah (principle):**
> *"The intention to commence an act that one is not currently in is valid and considered. The intention to commence an act that one is already in is invalid (lughw) and disregarded."*

- If in Ẓuhr he intends ʿAṣr → considered (he is not in ʿAṣr), so he moves out of Ẓuhr into ʿAṣr. His Ẓuhr becomes invalid.
- If in Ẓuhr he intends Ẓuhr again → not considered (he is already in Ẓuhr), so the intention is surplus/void. He simply continues his Ẓuhr as if nothing happened. His previous rakʿah remains valid.

**Practical implication:**
If a person wants to restart his Ṣalāh from the beginning, he should either:
1. Do something that breaks his Ṣalāh, exit, then restart; or
2. Make a new taḥrīmah (Allāhu Akbar with raised hands) to enter a different Ṣalāh.

Simply making a new niyyah for the same Ṣalāh he is already in has no legal effect.

## Definitions / Terminology

- **Mufsid (مفسد):** A nullifier — something that renders the Ṣalāh invalid
- **Kālam al-nās (كلام الناس):** Human speech; the communication of meaning between people
- **Sāhī (ساهي):** Oversight — something leaves the mind momentarily and returns with the slightest reminder
- **Nisyān (نسيان):** Forgetfulness — something previously known leaves the memory entirely and requires reacquisition
- **Khataʾ (خطأ):** Mistake — the knowledge remained but the action went wrong (e.g., swallowing water while knowing one is fasting)
- **Ḥurūf al-Zawāʾid (حروف الزوائد):** Supplementary/extra letters in Arabic morphology, gathered in the mnemonic اليوم تنساه (alif, lām, yāʾ, wāw, mīm, tāʾ, nūn, sīn, alif, hāʾ)
- **Laqmah / Istiftāḥ (لقمة / استفتاح):** Prompting or correcting a reciter who is stuck; technically, giving a laqmah to someone other than one's own Imam
- **Tashmiyt al-ʿĀṭis (تشميت العاطس):** Responding to someone's sneeze by saying *yarḥamukallāh*
- **Tanḥnuḥ (تنحنح):** Clearing the throat
- **Jazaʿ wa Taʾassuf (الجزع والتأسف):** Anguish and regret; expression of these through sounds during Ṣalāh is treated as human speech
- **Mufṣaʿ / Mufṣiʿ (مفصع):** The dalīl or basis of an argument (as used in the ʿibārah)
- **Lughw (لغو):** Surplus/void — a legal act that carries no weight or consequence
- **Istiḥsān (استحسان):** Juristic preference — adopting a ruling that departs from strict qiyās for a stronger reason
- **Tarafayn (الطرفان):** Imam Abū Ḥanīfah and Imam Muḥammad ibn al-Ḥasan al-Shaybānī
- **Ṣāḥibayn (الصاحبان):** Imam Abū Yūsuf and Imam Muḥammad
- **Talaqqī min al-Khārij (التلقي من الخارج):** Receiving/accepting a laqmah from someone outside the Ṣalāh

## Examples Given

- **Khataʾ in Ṣalāh:** A person knows he is praying but something accidentally slips out of his mouth — he didn't intend to say anything, but it came out while he was aware he was in Ṣalāh.
- **Khataʾ in fasting:** Knowing one is fasting but accidentally swallowing water while gargling.
- **"Āh" vs "Ūḥ":** Per Imam Abū Yūsuf, "āh" (alif + hāʾ, both zawāʾid) does not break Ṣalāh; "ūḥ" (three letters) always does.
- **"Īh" (إيه):** Used in Pakistan to express astonishment — per al-Ṭarafayn this communicates a meaning and breaks Ṣalāh even though both letters could be from zawāʾid.
- **"Min" (من):** A two-letter word with a complete meaning (from) — breaks Ṣalāh per al-Ṭarafayn.
- **Single letter expressing annoyance:** E.g., in a long Ṣalāh, a Muqtadī sighing "t!" when the Imam doesn't go to rukūʿ — if it communicates annoyance by common usage, it breaks Ṣalāh.
- **Clearing throat to continue recitation:** An Imam clears his throat to remove phlegm so the congregation can hear clearly — this is a valid excuse.
- **Responding to children during Ṣalāh:** Better to say *subḥānallāh* loudly, or recite one's current tasbīḥ loudly, rather than making throat-clearing sounds to alert them.
- **Jumping from Ẓuhr to ʿAṣr mid-Ṣalāh:** Prayed one rakʿah of Ẓuhr, then formed intention for ʿAṣr and said Allāhu Akbar without raising hands — Ẓuhr is rendered invalid, ʿAṣr continues for four rakʿāt.
- **Intending Ẓuhr again while already in Ẓuhr:** The niyyah has no effect; he simply continues his Ẓuhr.
- **Wrong laqmah:** If a Muqtadī gives an incorrect laqmah and the Imam takes it — per the matn position, both Ṣalāhs are affected; per the fatwā of Durr al-Mukhtār, correcting one's Imam in any scenario does not break Ṣalāh.
- **Yarḥamukallāh vs Yarḥamunīallāh:** The former (customary response) breaks Ṣalāh; the latter (not customary) does not.

## Questions / Unclear Points

- The exact line between "communicates a meaning" and "does not communicate a meaning" for short utterances was discussed but remains somewhat contextual — it depends on the ʿurf (custom) of the people speaking that language.
- How Imam Abū Yūsuf's rule of ḥurūf al-zawāʾid applies to non-Arabic languages was raised but not fully resolved; the teacher indicated the rule was conceived in an Arabic context and is one of the reasons al-Ṭarafayn's approach is stronger.
- The exact ruling in the case of the Muqtadī who gives a wrong laqmah to his Imam (and whether this breaks anyone's Ṣalāh) involves a difference between the matn position and the fatwā position — students should be aware of both.
- Whether laxity given for Nawāfil applies to Tarāwīḥ: confirmed that Tarāwīḥ is a Sunnah Muʾakkadah (close to wājib in ruling) and does *not* benefit from the laxity specific to Nawāfil.
- The final two nullifiers (8 and 9 — excessive movement and receiving laqmah from outside) were only named and not yet explained in detail.
